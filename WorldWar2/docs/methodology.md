# Methodology

This page explains how the claims in `data/claims/` were built and how to read them. The column definitions are in [../schema/README.md](../schema/README.md).

## Claims and event-based rows

One row is one claim: *unit X was at place P on date D, doing A, and here is the source.* Rows are **event-based**, not daily. A unit gets a row on days when something substantial happened (landing, arrival, capture of a place, a major attack or counterattack, a command change, a headquarters move, a surrender, destruction). Quiet days are intentionally absent, so a unit's position between two rows is unknown, not unchanged. Do not interpolate unless you say so.

Each row has:

- **Who**: `unit_id` (stable key, one file per unit), plus `unit_name`, `echelon`, `branch` copied from `data/units.csv`.
- **When / where**: `date`, `theater`, `place_id` (a row of `data/places.csv`; see "Places" below), `location_qualifier`, the claim's coordinates (equal to the place's unless `coord_override` says why), `location_represents` (headquarters, main body, sector centre, front-line point, objective, approach route), `precision_km`.
- **What**: `event_type` (one of 18 values), `current_activity` (short free-text phrase), `engaged_with` (the unit ids of the opponents fought that day; `engaged_note` for an opponent that is not a unit, such as an air force).
- **Command**: `parent_unit_id` (operational parent on that date), the command relations (the master table `data/command_relations.csv`, linked to the claims by `data/command_relation_claims.csv`: dated links organic, assigned, attached, unspecified_subordination, operational/administrative control, supporting, coordinating and reserve; see "Command relations table" below), `commander` on that date (time-dependent), `composing_units` (listed when first given or when it changes).
- **Strength**: optional `strength_json` (numbers and/or a qualitative `status`); `strength_status` is derived from it (see "Derived values" below).
- **Quality**: `confidence`, `claim_status`, `source`, `notes`.

Rows were built by reading the sources in [sources.md](sources.md), writing one claim per unit-date, geocoding the place (Wikipedia coordinates first, OpenStreetMap Nominatim for gaps), then assigning precision, confidence and the other fields by the rules below.

## Units, battle groups and parents

Every unit has one `unit_id` and one row in `data/units.csv`. Battle groups (Kampfgruppen) and combat commands are units of their own (`echelon` = `regiment/battle_group`) with a fixed `parent_unit_id` in `data/units.csv` (the organic parent division) and, on each claim, a time-dependent `parent_unit_id` and `command_relationships` (for example attached to Panzer Lehr, with the organic parent division kept in the list). Brigades stay brigades. A battalion that is only an opponent or a component is a placeholder with `echelon` = `battalion` (rank 1, below `regiment/battle_group`); regiments and battle groups share `regiment/battle_group`. Neither carries an echelon letter in the id (vocabulary `echelon_id_letter`, `schema/README.md` "unit_id pattern"), and the validator rejects an id whose final echelon letter contradicts the echelon (review item 6). When all of a division's rows were really about its battle group, the division has no claim file and is a `parent_only` unit so that parent links still resolve. A claim is never duplicated between a division and its battle group.

## Placeholder units

`data/units.csv` also holds **placeholder** units: identity records created so that every ground formation of the Western Front (6 June 1944 to 8 May 1945: northwest Europe plus southern France/Dragoon, not Italy) has a stable `unit_id` before anyone researches it. This lets claims, `command_relationships` and `engaged_with` names point at real records, and `build/coverage.csv` / `docs/coverage.md` show what is still to do.

- **A placeholder is not a claim.** It says nothing about where or when a unit was anywhere. It has `record_status = placeholder` and no claim file. `record_status` is `active` when a claim file exists (`partial` is reserved for a claim file known to be incomplete). When you add a claim file for a placeholder, run `python scripts/promote_placeholders.py`; the validator rejects a claim file for a placeholder.
- **Scope of the first pass.** Army group, army, corps and division level, plus significant independent brigades (British, Canadian and Polish armoured and tank brigades, commando brigades, the Belgian, Dutch and Czechoslovak brigades) and the German Panzer-Brigaden that fought in the West. Not yet included: regiments, Kampfgruppen (except those that already have claims), separate US tank, tank destroyer and artillery groups, German Heeres and Korps troops, fortress and Sicherungs units, naval and air units (see `docs/roadmap.md`). The 1945 Volks-Artillerie-Korps are included as corps. No US Marine Corps ground formation served on the Western Front in this period; Royal Marines are part of the commando brigades.
- **Where the list comes from.** Allied units: the order-of-battle pages on English Wikipedia (12th and 6th Army Group 8 May 1945, 21st Army Group, Normandy, Market Garden, Bulge, Dragoon, Nordwind). German units: the 6 June 1944 order of battle on German Wikipedia (citing Niehorster and Jentz), the Lexikon der Wehrmacht division, corps, army and army-group pages and their timelines (a unit is included when its timeline shows a Western Front entry between 6 June 1944 and 8 May 1945), and the same Wikipedia order-of-battle pages for Dragoon, Market Garden, Bulge and Nordwind. `listing_source` records which of these listed the unit.
- **One record per lineage page.** Renamed or reconstituted units are not merged: for example the 352nd Infantry Division and the 352nd Volksgrenadier Division, or a Reserve Corps and the Armeekorps it became, are separate records with a `unit_note`. The existing ids and names are unchanged. Since 2 November 2026 the link itself is data: one row of `data/unit_lineage.csv` per predecessor and successor record (kind `redesignation`, `reconstitution_from_remnants`, `absorbed_into` or `formed_from_remnants`, with date, date basis and references; schema/README.md). A link is added only when a source (Lexikon der Wehrmacht, which follows Tessin) states continuity: the new formation is the old one renamed, or was formed with its remnants. A new division that only reuses a destroyed division's number, formed from another formation with no remnants named, gets no link; its `unit_note` may say so. Lineage rows are never cycles: when a unit's number passes back (for example a brigade returning to its parent division), the return is described in `unit_note` only.
- **`formed_date` is formation or activation, not arrival in theater.** It is filled only where a source states it plainly: for German units the Lexikon der Wehrmacht page gives the date in its first sentence (a date that is only an order to form, as for the 1944 Panzer-Brigaden or the two new SS corps, is left blank); for Allied units it is the start date in the Wikipedia infobox, used only when the span begins in 1939-1945 (units whose infobox begins with a First World War lineage are left blank for later work). Infobox dates marked with an asterisk may be constitution rather than activation dates; each such row says so in `unit_note`. `formed_date_source` is the page used; precision is the day, the month or the year as the source gives it. When a record is named for a later designation of the same staff (for example a Division Nr. reorganised as an infantry division, a reserve or grenadier division renamed, a brigade enlarged to a division), `formed_date` is the formation of the staff, so that rows under the earlier designation fall inside the record's life; the designation date is a `redesignation` claim row where the source gives a place, and a "Formed date" sentence in `unit_note` (consistency fix 1, 9 November 2026).
- **`destruction` means the unit ended.** A row where a unit is overrun, broken into pockets or reduced to a cipher but the sources follow it afterwards is a `defense` or `attack` row, not `destruction`; a later `activation` row starts a second formation under the same record. Map rows that still show a unit after a text source ends it are `alternate` in a conflict group with the text row (consistency fix 1, 9 November 2026).
- **`initial_parent_*` is a listing, not a command relationship.** Where a source clearly lists a unit under a formation, `initial_parent_unit_id`, `initial_parent_as_of` and `initial_parent_source` record it: for German units the 6 June 1944 order of battle (`DEW6`, as of 1944-06-06) or, for units that arrived later, the first Western Front entry in the Lexikon timeline whose corps matches a unit in the table (`LEX`, as of that month; the unit may have arrived earlier); for the VI Corps units of Operation Dragoon the Wikipedia Dragoon page. Allied placeholders otherwise have no parent: their attachments change from week to week and need dated sources. These columns do not say organic or attached and are never used for `command_relationships`; the existing `parent_unit_id` column keeps its meaning (organic parent of a battle group).
- **Opposing units are ids.** `engaged_with` is a `;` list of `unit_id`s (foreign key to `data/units.csv`), `unknown` when combat had no identified opponent, blank when there was none. An opponent that is a named unit but not in the table is added as a placeholder first (C0072 and others name the 6th Fallschirmjäger Regiment, C0096 and C0097 the SS Heavy Panzer Battalion 101); an opponent that cannot be a unit (an air force, naval forces, an unnamed formation: 'British forces (Gold Beach sector; unit not named by CMH)') goes verbatim in `engaged_note`, never an invented id. The readable names are derived into `build/all_claims.csv` (`engaged_with_names`, `<adjective> <unit_name>`, then the notes). The validator rejects an unknown id, a duplicate, the claiming unit itself, an opponent on the same side, and a note that repeats the name of a unit that has an id.

- **Promoting a placeholder (Scott, 9 November 2026).** A placeholder gets its first claims from at least two independent source families wherever possible (the Lexikon der Wehrmacht/Tessin is one family; CMH, FMS, NARA captured records, Ellis, Stacey, de Lattre and the 12th Army Group maps are others; geocoders do not count). When only one family covers the unit, its rows are entered at confidence 0.3 and each says why in the notes (which sources were searched and found nothing). Lexikon table rows (army corps, army, army group, place by month) are 0.3 rows dated to the 15th of the month at a representative point; a dated Lexikon sentence with a place is 0.5, 0.7 with a second family, 0.9 with a second family and an exact place. A renaming that keeps the unit in the same record (the record's name gives both titles) is not an end; a renaming into a different record, or into a unit with no record yet, is `disestablished`.
- **Other fronts and periods (Scott, 9 November 2026).** "As you review a source, keep in mind that we may use it for more than this period of time (if applicable). Keep notes and data even if it applies to other fronts and time periods. This is a general rule that applies to every resource analysis." Rows before 6 June 1944 on any front are entered under `OP-PRE-INVASION`; because the claim's `theater` must repeat the unit record (`western_europe`), each such row carries the sentence "Other front or period: ..." in its notes. Rows on other fronts after 5 June 1944 (for example the Eastern Front, Slovakia, Hungary, Italy) are kept in the private notes and the roadmap until an umbrella operation and a per-claim theater exist (roadmap 362). Home and occupied rear areas (Germany, Denmark, the Netherlands, Poland within the 1939 Reich) after 5 June 1944 take the campaign whose dates cover the day, with a sentence saying the place is a rear area, not that campaign's battlefield. Country codes are added to the `country` vocabulary as places in new countries appear.

## Flak and coastal artillery

Flak units and coastal artillery are ground units whatever service owned them: Luftwaffe flak corps, divisions, regiments and battle groups, and Kriegsmarine coastal batteries and naval coastal artillery battalions such as Marine-Artillerie-Abteilung 260 (Scott, 4 and 13 November 2026; standing rule 23 in `docs/HANDOFF.md`). They hold ground positions, so their rows are in the main outputs and the who_controls grid. The `branch` column still records the service. The `domain` values `air` and `naval` are for flying units, air commands, ships and naval commands only.

## Out-of-scope claims

A claim for a unit outside the current scope (any domain, front or period) is recorded when a source gives it, because it will help as the scope grows (Scott, 13 November 2026; standing rule 22 in `docs/HANDOFF.md`; it builds on the rule to keep data for all fronts and periods above). It follows every normal claim rule and stays out of the main outputs and the who_controls grid, as air and naval claims do today in `build/other_domains/`. Coverage and source-balance figures for out-of-scope areas are labelled **incidental, not surveyed**: they show what the sources happened to give, not a systematic search, and are not compared with the surveyed Western Front figures. The unit `scope` marker (`out_of_scope`) and the unit `domain` separate them (built in the schema pass of 13 November 2026; `docs/air_naval_pilot.md` section 8).

## Air and naval units

Air and naval units are collected beside the ground data (domain `air` or `naval`; `build/other_domains/`), and a unit outside the current scope can be marked `scope` `out_of_scope` (standing rule 22; the schema pass of 13 November 2026). Ships are units with echelon `naval_unit`; a ship's type, hull number and class (`ship_type`, `hull_number`, `ship_class_id`) are columns of the unit, and the class holds the as-designed figures (`data/ship_classes.csv`), each with its own source and none entered without one. A ship's refit is a dated, sourced override on a claim (`strength_json` `capabilities`, basis `refit`); its answer for a date is the latest refit on or before it, else the class figures. A reclassification is a `redesignation` claim. Naval formations use the naval echelon ladder (`naval_unit` < `naval_division` < `naval_squadron` < `task_group` < `fleet`, ranks 10-14, no id letter); a task organisation is a placeholder that holds relations and no claims. A position at sea is a `sea_position` place with confidence at most 0.5; an airfield or port a unit operates from is `location_represents` `base`; `commissioning` and `sortie` are event types. The ship-class figures seeded on 13 November 2026 are the English Wikipedia infobox text as written (family `wikipedia`, secondary, medium); armament lines are sorted into main, secondary, anti-aircraft and torpedo by keyword, so a class note names the page to read for the exact fit. Details: `docs/air_naval_pilot.md` section 8.

Air units have their own echelon ladder (`air_flight` to `air_force`, ranks 19-25, no id letter; `air_flight`, for flights, servicing echelons and similar elements below a squadron, was added on 19 November 2026), ranked by level rather than by national name (a RAF wing is the level of a US group, a RAF group the level of a US combat wing). Aircraft types are rows of `data/aircraft_types.csv` (as-published performance figures for one named variant, each with its own source; nothing is estimated), and a unit's aircraft on hand and serviceable are a dated record in `strength_json` (`aircraft`; the figures of the Luftwaffe and USAAF returns as the source states them). An air unit's administrative and operational parents are both recorded (`assigned` or `administrative_control`, and `attached` or `operational_control`) and are not a conflict (`docs/air_naval_pilot.md` section 9).

## Irregular forces

Resistance groups, partisan bands and other irregular forces get unit entries only once they are organised into formal military units with a command chain (Scott, 12 November 2026). The Dutch Stoottroepen, formed from the Binnenlandse Strijdkrachten, are an example: they had a formal command, named battalions, and served under Allied formations, so they are entered. Loose cells and networks (for example the Dutch KP, OD and RVV) are not entered as units, even when they had a paper organisation or a regional commander. Existence, dates and strength figures for organisations that are not entered stay in the private notes. Formal units still follow the small-unit rule: a formation below regiment size needs a parent traced to a division or higher, unless it is an independent unit of regiment size or larger (`docs/HANDOFF.md` standing rules 17 and 21; `schema/README.md`).


## US Army fill-in (Batch 1, July to September 1944)

Army group, army, corps and division claims for the US Army on the Western Front are being filled in by period, with a spot check between batches. Batch 1 covers 1 July to 30 September 1944 (Cobra, Brittany and Brest, Mortain, Falaise, Paris, the pursuit, the Siegfried Line approach, Lorraine, and Dragoon). Rules specific to this work:

- **Theater per claim row (decision Scott, 9 November 2026: "For the out of theatre data, I do want to keep those").** `theater` is no longer repeated from `data/units.csv`: each claim row has the theater of its own place and date, and its operation must have the same theater. Rows outside the Western Front operations are filed under one holding umbrella per front (`OP-FRONT-EASTERN`, `OP-FRONT-MEDITERRANEAN`, `OP-FRONT-BALKANS`, `OP-FRONT-SCANDINAVIA`, `OP-FRONT-NORTH-AFRICA`) or `OP-HOME-GERMANY` for rear and home rows inside the Reich of the time; they need no CMH campaign streamer. **Backfill (9 November 2026, `/workspace` script, deterministic):** only rows that carried the old note sentence "Other front or period: ..." (24 rows) or "Rear-area row after 5 June 1944: ..." (3 rows) were looked at; all other rows stayed `western_europe`. By the country of the place: RU, UA, LT, MD -> `eastern_front`; DE, AT, CZ -> `home_germany` (all such rows are forming, training or refitting before the front reached them); DK -> `scandinavia`; FR, BE, NL, LU -> `western_europe` (the 1940 campaign and the occupation); PL -> by the German border of 31 December 1937: Groß-Born, Wandern and Rügenwalde (inside) `home_germany`, Białystok (outside) `eastern_front`. Result: 10 `eastern_front` (106th Infantry Division 1941-1944), 9 `home_germany` (106th at Wahn; 242nd at Groß-Born; 265th and 19th Luftwaffe Field at Bergen; 266th at Münsingen; 343rd at Grafenwöhr; 157th Reserve at Munich; Volks-Artillerie-Korps 405 at Rügenwalde; 257th Volksgrenadier at Wandern), 1 `scandinavia` (9th Volksgrenadier at Esbjerg), 7 unchanged `western_europe` (XXV Corps 1940-1942). Each row's note sentence was replaced by a `Context:` sentence with the old and new theater and operation. New rows from then on take their theater by the same rules.
- **Theater.** Southern France from the Dragoon landing on 15 August 1944 is `western_europe` (the Western Front scope includes the Riviera-to-Rhine campaign); the theater vocabulary was amended to say so. Pre-landing rows for the 3rd, 36th and 45th Divisions (staging in Italy) are out of scope and have no claims.
- **Rows are milestones, not daily positions:** arrival or landing, first combat, major attacks, defences, captures and surrenders, relocations, command changes, attachments and detachments, and HQ moves for corps and above. The regiment level and combat commands are not in this batch.
- **Division rows from the CMH ETO-OB pages** (see `docs/sources.md`) come in five kinds: arrival or landing, first combat, corps assignment change, commander change and command-post (CP) rows near fixed dates (25 July, 31 July, 7, 15 and 25 August, 2, 12 and 20 September and 30 September, within two days). A CP row is `hq_move` with `location_represents` = `headquarters` and says only that CMH lists the CP at that town. A promotion without a change of commander (3rd Armored Division, 8th and 90th Divisions) is recorded as `other`, not `command_change`.
- **Place and confidence for CP-derived rows** depend on the gap in days between the row date and the CMH CP date nearest to it: same day 0.7 and 3 km; 1-2 days 0.5 and 10 km; 3-5 days 0.3 and 25 km; more than 5 days 0.3 and 50 km (rows more than 14 days from any CP are dropped). A CP given only as "vicinity" or with an offset caps confidence at 0.5 and precision at 10 km or worse. Corps-change rows cap confidence at 0.5. The CP date and gap are stated in `notes`. Arrival rows use the nearest Continental CP and the row says how far from the arrival date it is.
- **Assignment table.** The CMH table has a single corps column and separate Army Assigned, Army Attached, Army Group Assigned and Army Group Attached columns. Army and army-group cells are recorded as `assigned` or `attached` as the column says; a suffix `(Opn)`/`(Opns)`/`(Operations)` makes the link `operational_control` and `(Adm)`/`(Sup)`/`(Admin)` makes it `administrative_control`. The single corps column does not say assigned or attached, so a corps link is `unspecified_subordination` with `effective_from` (the table date) and `date_basis` `assignment_table`, unless a Green Book passage names the relationship (then `attached`/`assigned` with `date_basis` `narrative`, for example the 35th Division attached to V Corps on 27 July 1944). Cells that are not units in `data/units.csv` (ETOUSA, UK Base, Com Z sections) are not recorded. Army Group columns use `US-AG-012G`, `US-AG-006G`, `UK-AG-021G` and `ALL-AFHQ-000H`. (Batch 1 first wrote the corps link as `attached` and the army as `organic`, then, after the 25 October 2026 review, every table link as `unspecified_subordination` with a note that the table did not separate the columns; that note was wrong for army and army-group columns and was corrected in Batch 2, which regenerated 213 table-derived rows.) Short flips (a corps change reversed within two days) are ignored. The start of a relation whose `valid_from_basis` is `assignment_table` is the first row of the unbroken run of table rows that list the higher unit in the matching column, not the date of the first claim that lists it; 54 relations were moved to that date on 26 October 2026 (V-013: the 8 firm cases, 7 army and army-group relations of the same units, the 13 that started 1 to 7 days late, the 17 that started 8 or more days late and 9 of the 57 whose table needed another reader); every `assignment_table` relation now starts on its table row except four that are open items (CR0105, CR0156, CR0200, CR0201), see `schema/README.md`, "Start of a table-based interval". In the 45th Division table the corps 'V' on 15 September is read as a transcription error for VI.
- **Geocoding of CMH places.** CMH CP towns are geocoded with Nominatim, and each result is checked against the département CMH gives (old names such as Seine-et-Oise are mapped to today's departments); results that fail the check are dropped or corrected by hand (for example Arpajon, Longnes and Le Ruel resolved to the right département; Aspères (Gard) for the 45th Division on 23 August was dropped because it conflicts with the division's route). CMH spellings are kept as the place's name or `alt_names` (`data/places.csv`).
- **Army group, army and corps points.** Where no source places the headquarters, the row uses a named representative point with `location_represents` = `sector_center`, 50 km precision, confidence 0.3 and `location_basis` `representative_point` (Seventh Army on 15 September: its headquarters on that date is not documented). The review found headquarters places for 12th Army Group on 1 August (Château des Mares, Saint-Sauveur-Lendelin), Third Army on 1 August (Néhou) and 6th Army Group on 15 September (Lyon, Palais de la Foire); those rows now point at the headquarters with `location_basis` set accordingly.
- **Source conflicts become paired rows** (`claim_status` preferred and alternate with one `conflict_group_id`): 4th Armored Division arrival (CMH 13 July, USHMM and Wikipedia 11 July; **dissolved on 26 October 2026**: the rows are two events, a landing and an arrival, and are now separate preferred rows), VIII Corps passing to Ninth Army (Breakout and Pursuit p636 gives 10 September; CMH division tables and Wikipedia give 5 September), and the fall of Brest (Green Book 18 September, Wikipedia 19 September). The review showed that these rows are partly different event definitions rather than rival dates: they now carry an `Event definition:` sentence in `notes` (see below).

## Parents and command relationships

`parent_unit_id` is the **operational parent** on the row's date, as one unit id from `data/units.csv`: the formation the unit was attached to or operating under, otherwise its standing parent. It is blank for a top-level headquarters, and blank when no parent could be verified; in that case the claim lists one `unknown` placeholder relation (no higher unit, relationship `unknown`, a note; the text `unknown` is not used as a value). The relations do not store the formation's name: it is derived from `units.csv` (`<nationality adjective> <unit_name>`, for example "US VII Corps"; multinational headquarters keep their own name), as in `higher_name` of `build/command_relations.csv`. The claim's relations also hold the standing parent (`organic`, `assigned` or `unspecified_subordination`), attachments (`attached`), formations with another kind of authority (`operational_control`, `administrative_control`, `supporting`, `coordinating`) and reserves (`reserve`), each with the dates the sources give (`valid_from`, `valid_to`, `date_basis`); see the `relationship` vocabulary. Rules used when the free text of v5 was converted (details in `scripts/migrate_parent_formation.py`):

- A plain parent in the old text became an `organic` entry. This recorded the standing parent the source names, not a claim that the link was permanent (corps and armies reshuffled divisions constantly). Since the review of 25 October 2026 `organic` is narrower (see below): the old entries for a division, corps or army under a higher headquarters were converted to `assigned` or `unspecified_subordination`.
- "attached to X" makes X the operational parent and the named parent an `organic` entry (battle groups attached to another division or corps).
- A corps or high-command reserve becomes `reserve`. US First Army under 21st Army Group is `operational_control`.
- Where the old text listed a battle group's division and a higher formation without saying how they relate, the division stays the parent unless the row's notes show who controlled the group; the higher formation is kept with relationship `unknown`.
- Parents named without an id (Fifteenth Army, OKW, SHAEF) were added to `units.csv` as placeholder units; SHAEF is nationality `Allied` (a multinational headquarters, see `data/nationalities.csv`).
- `unknown` is kept (as an entry, not as the text value of `parent_unit_id`) where the source did not give a parent; the migration adds no relationship that the old text or the row's notes do not support.

**Operational parent versus organic parent (rule).** `parent_unit_id` answers "who commanded this unit **on this row's date**"; the standing (organic) parent is never put only in `parent_unit_id`: it is recorded as a command relation of the claim (`organic`), and a temporary parent as `attached` (or `operational_control`, `reserve`). So a row for an independent brigade that landed under a division's command has the division as `parent_unit_id` (relationship `attached`) and the army or corps that holds the brigade as the standing (`assigned`) relation; it is not an organic part of the division (review finding, C0001). Three further rules:

- **Dates decide.** An attachment applies from the day a source says it took effect, not from the day of the event it enabled. Put that day in the entry's optional `effective_from`, and keep a row's relationship in line with its own date: the 82nd Airborne's bridgehead attack was ordered for 17 June under VII Corps, and control passed to VIII Corps on 19 June (C0131, C0141).
- **Record "unknown", do not guess.** If a source gives the attachment but not the standing parent, add an entry with relationship `unknown` (with `unit_id` null and a note saying what is unknown; the validator warns about an `attached` entry with no standing entry (`organic`, `assigned`, `unspecified_subordination` or `unknown`) beside it).
- **A relationship is not an organisation chart.** "Under command for the assault" or "attached" does not make the unit part of the other formation; `organic` is reserved for constituted components (a division's own regiments, a battle group's own division); a standing parent that is a higher headquarters is `assigned`.

## Command relations table

Command is stored once, as a table, and the claims are its evidence (October 2026, branch `command-relations-table`; the design and its columns are in `schema/README.md`, "Command relations").

- **Why.** Before, the same relation was repeated in every claim that restated it in a JSON cell (1,076 entries for 348 distinct subject, higher unit and relationship triples, 217 KB of the claim files), the intervals were rebuilt on every build for US units in one window only, and a gap such as "no source dates the end" or "no unit is named" had no row to carry it.
- **What a relation is.** One interval of one relationship between a subordinate unit and a higher unit (`data/command_relations.csv`, ids `CR0001`...). The claims that list it are in `data/command_relation_claims.csv` with the dates each claim itself stated (`stated_*`); the interval is the merge of those statements. The old interval logic is unchanged: consecutive preferred claims listing the same (higher unit, relationship) form one run, a sourced `effective_to` closes it, otherwise it ends the day before the next claim that no longer lists it (`inferred_from_next_claim`, with the last listing as `valid_to_earliest`), otherwise it stays open at the unit's latest claim.
- **Nothing is invented.** A relation that no source dates keeps `valid_from_basis` `claim_date` (the first claim that lists it) and `end_unknown` is `true` for a temporary relationship (attached, operational control, reserve, administrative control) whose end is only inferred or still open. The `unknown` placeholders (31 claim entries with no named higher unit, 21 intervals) are rows with a blank higher unit and a note. The five undated German parent changes of June 1944 (21st Panzer, 12th SS Panzer, Panzer Lehr, I SS Panzer Corps, II SS Panzer Corps) are flagged `change_undated`: both relations keep the dates they have and the change lies between the last listing of the first and the first listing of the second.
- **A claim of the higher unit can be evidence (review item 1).** A relation is normally evidenced by claims of its subject. Where the subject has no claim of its own (a placeholder such as the 6th Fallschirmjäger Regiment) or the relation is only stated by the formation it belongs to, the link in `data/command_relation_claims.csv` has role `listed_by_higher_unit`: the claim of the higher unit lists the subject in `composing_units`, the link keeps the claim's wording in `stated_note`, and the link is not one of the listing claim's own relationships. A relationship whose subordinate formation is not a unit of the table at all (a regiment, battalion or squadron) is not a relation yet: it is kept, with the claim's date and source as stated, in `docs/deferred_relations.md` until that formation becomes a unit.
- **Overlaps.** Different relationship classes and echelons overlap by design (a division assigned to an army, attached to a corps, with an organic or `unknown` relation beside them). A contradictory overlap (same relationship class, different higher units of one echelon) is allowed only if documented in `data/command_relation_overlaps.csv`; the validator rejects an unlisted or stale one. The 19 listed overlaps are cases where the sources date a transfer differently, or one end is inferred from a later claim; none is resolved.
- **Relations open at the end of the war (Scott, 9 November 2026).** For Allied units, a relation still in force on 8 May 1945 counts as ended there: it keeps `open_at_last_claim`, and nothing is added to close it. A relation is a repair candidate only when its unit has rows more than 90 days after its last listing that do not list it (command-chain repair, branch `wf-health-fixes`, 9 November 2026).
- **Carry-forward cutoff in `who_controls` (13 November 2026).** An `open_at_last_claim` interval is answered as `possible` for at most 90 days after its `last_listed_date` (`OPEN_CARRY_FORWARD_DAYS` in `scripts/who_controls.py`). Beyond that the interval no longer covers the date (the answer is dropped). This is a reading cutoff, not an invented end: the master table is unchanged, sourced ends are never capped, an inferred end stays `possible` only inside its already bounded interval, and a later relation that still covers the date continues to answer. 90 days is the same threshold already used for stale-relation repair candidates. 60 days was considered and rejected, because on VE-Day it would also drop late-war divisions last listed only 61-89 days earlier that were still in action in April and May. Allied relations still in force on 8 May stay `open_at_last_claim` in the tables; the cutoff does not add an end row for them.
- **Organic fallback in `who_controls` (14 November 2026).** Old behaviour: `organic` relations are not in `build/command_relations.csv`, so a unit with only an organic relation on a date (for example Combat Command A of the 2nd Armored Division after the 90 days of its XIX Corps listing) had no answer. New behaviour: where no non-organic relation of any kind covers the date at a level of the chain, the lookup falls back to the organic parent (from the new `build/organic_relations.csv`) and goes on up the chain as normal; an explicit non-organic relation, even a stale `possible` one, always wins; the organic relation obeys the same 90-day rule; the answer is marked `organic_fallback`. Reason: an organic parent is a sourced fact, and a combat command or Kampfgruppe was under its division's chain of command unless a source says otherwise. No data changed. See `schema/README.md`, "Reading a command interval", and scenario 266.
- **`parent_unit_id` stays on the claim.** It is the editorial operational parent on the claim's date and 41 of 566 values cannot be derived by a rule (judgement between attached, operational control and standing parents), so it is validated against the claim's relations instead of derived: it must be the higher unit of one of them.
- **Where things live.** The claim files no longer have a `command_relationships` column. `common.load_all_claims()` rebuilds that text for the scripts and `build/all_claims.csv` carries it as a derived column; `build/command_relations.csv` is derived from the master table for all nationalities (356 rows, 21 of them placeholders) and read by `scripts/who_controls.py`.
- **Migration check.** `scripts/migrate_command_relations.py` ran once; `--verify 54933a8` rebuilds the former per-claim text from the tables and compares it with the claim files of the commit before the migration: 566 of 566 claims match (450 byte for byte, 116 differ only in the key order inside an entry, which is now canonical) and the derived table for the old window matches the old `build/command_relations.csv` row for row. `who_controls.py` gives the same answer as before on every query that had an answer.

## Command relationships and location dates (review of Batch 1)

The 15-row spot check of Batch 1 (reviewed by a second reviewer and verified against the Green Books, the 6th Army Group G-3 report, Wikimanche and other sources, see [`review/review_changes.md`](review/review_changes.md)) led to these rules. They are additive and backward compatible: the old values are still allowed, but the validator warns where `organic` is used wrongly.

**Relationship vocabulary.**
- `organic`: a constituted component (regiment or battalion of its own division or regiment; a battle group's own division). Not used for a division, corps or army under a higher headquarters.
- `assigned`: the source says assigned, or lists the unit as a standing member of the headquarters' order of battle without attachment language (army to army group, corps to army, division to corps where a narrative source says so).
- `attached`: a temporary or task-organised subordination, only where the source says attached.
- `unspecified_subordination`: the source shows the unit under the headquarters but does not distinguish assigned from attached. In the CMH ETO-OB assignment tables only the corps column is combined, so table-derived corps links use it (army and army-group columns are separate). It is never converted to `attached` or `assigned` without a source.
- `operational_control`, `administrative_control`, `supporting`, `coordinating`: other kinds of authority. 6th Army Group on 15 September 1944 has SHAEF with operational control (tactical control passed from AFHQ at 0001B), while AFHQ's retained supply, administrative and civil-affairs responsibility "in the area overrun" is a theater function and is listed as `unknown` with a scoped note, not as administrative control; 12th Army Group on 1 August has SHAEF as the organisational parent (`assigned`) and 21st Army Group `coordinating` until 31 August, because Montgomery coordinated both army groups until Eisenhower assumed direct operational control on 1 September.
- Entries may carry `effective_from`, `effective_to` (last day) and `date_basis` (`order`, `assignment_table`, `narrative`, `inferred`). A table date is the date the table shows, which can precede the day command actually passed (VIII Corps to Ninth Army: 5 September in the tables, 10 September in the Green Book).

**Location date and basis.** A coordinate taken from a command-post (CP) list is only evidence for the date of that list. Two optional columns record this: `location_date` (the date the coordinate is documented for) and `location_basis` (`documented_on_date`, `nearest_later_command_post`, `nearest_earlier_command_post`, `representative_point`, `town_centroid`, `operational_area`, `inferred`). Temporal bases come first: a point from a CP three days later is `nearest_later_command_post` whatever the place is. A town named for a headquarters whose site is not given is `town_centroid`. A sector, landing area or bridgehead is `operational_area`. There is no location confidence column: use a wider `precision_km` and `confidence` 0.5 or 0.3 where the row's own date is not documented. For Batch 1 rows the columns were backfilled from the notes (the CP-list wording); older rows are left blank except where a review found the answer.

**Landing, HQ and front-line points.** A landing row's point is the landing area or drop zone, not a headquarters or town centroid (the validator errors on `town_centroid` or a CP from another date). A corps or army sector is not an exact front-line point: use `sector_center` with 10 km or wider (the validator warns). A corps, army or army-group headquarters that is only a town centroid needs `precision_km` of at least 5. A named higher headquarters needs a stable `unit_id` (First Allied Airborne Army, British I Airborne Corps and British XXX Corps were added or linked for this).

**Event definition.** When the rows of a conflict group describe different events (a landing against an arrival, an administrative assignment date against an operational transfer, one capitulation against a commander's surrender) say so in `notes` with a sentence starting `Event definition:` on every row of the group; the validator warns when the event types differ and a row lacks it. Each source for a different kind of statement is cited in a labelled sentence: `Event date:`, `Relationship:`, `Location:`, `Coordinates:`.

**Event types added:** `activation` (a formation becomes operational or is constituted; the Ninth Army, 12th Army Group, Third Army and 6th Army Group rows) and `redesignation` (the same formation takes a new name; XVIII Corps to XVIII Airborne Corps on 25 August 1944, same identity).

**Geocoding failures found by the audit.** The CMH place "Mesnil-St-Martin (1 mi SW)" (4th Armored Division on 13 July 1944, 6th Armored Division on 18 July) resolved to a hamlet near Percy, about 50 km from the landing beaches and inconsistent with the next CP at Méautis. The three rows (C0244, C0245, C0269) keep the point at 50 km and confidence 0.3 (C0244 as `inferred`; the other two keep their nearest-command-post basis) until the place is identified.

## precision_km

`precision_km` is a **judgment estimate of the radius, in kilometres, within which the unit (or its main body) probably was**. It is not a surveyed error. Values are banded: 1, 5, 10, 25, 50 (and 3 once, as a manual override for a village-scale point). Starting rules (then reviewed row by row):

| location quality | division or smaller | corps | army and above |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact (named place tied to the date) | 1 | 5 | 5 |
| approximate | 5 | 10 | 25 |
| inferred | 10 | 25 | 50 |

Special cases: headquarters rows are 1 (exact), 5 (named town or village, approximate) or 25 (inferred); beach `sector_center` rows are at least 10 because the beaches are 8-10 km wide; `approach_route` rows are 25 (a march within a region) or 50 (a long move such as from Brittany or Chartres). Manual overrides are explained in the row's `notes`.

## Confidence rubric

`confidence` is a number applied by rule from location quality and the number of independent source families (Wikipedia, the US Army CMH/HyperWar volumes, and each other website count as separate families; several Wikipedia pages count as one family):

| value | rule |
|---|---|
| 0.9 | exact location and two independent source families agree |
| 0.7 | exact location with one authoritative source (for example CMH), or approximate location with two source families |
| 0.5 | exact location with one secondary source (Wikipedia, local-history page), or approximate location with one source family |
| 0.3 | position inferred or a representative point (HQ not tied to the date, unverified beach, front-line representative point), or the date itself is approximate |

**Corroboration rule (3 November 2026, approved by Scott).** When a second reference from a different `source_family` (geocoders excluded) is added to an existing row and agrees on unit, date and place, the row is raised to 0.7 if it was lower, with old value, new value and reason in `notes`. It does not apply when both references are in the same family (for example a CMH Combat Chronicle and a CMH Green Book), to rows in a conflict group, or to rows named in an open map-conflict point in `docs/roadmap.md` until Scott decides that point. Rows that already had two families when built keep the confidence they were given by the other rules (for example the 0.5 cap on ww2byday compilations); the rule is not applied retroactively to them.

Caps: the preferred claim of a conflict group is at most 0.7; alternate and disputed claims at most 0.5. Confidence measures the claim's date/place support, not the importance of the unit. Only these four levels are used, so scores are comparable.

## Conflict policy

Sources disagree. The dataset keeps every competing claim as its own row with its own source, rather than choosing silently:

- Rows share a `conflict_group_id` (`G001`, ...). A group has at least two claims about the **same unit**.
- `claim_status = preferred`: a non-conflicted claim, or the best-supported claim of a group (exactly one per group).
- `alternate`: a competing claim judged less likely than the preferred one.
- `disputed`: no source is judged better. A group with no preferred row is all disputed.
- Every non-conflicted row is `preferred`. `scripts/validate.py` enforces these rules.
- Each row's `notes` explains the disagreement and cites only the sources that support that row's own claim.

Minor source disagreements that do not change a row's date or place (for example a rank spelling) are recorded in `notes`, not as a conflict group. A few of these are listed in the roadmap's known-gaps section.

## strength_json

An optional JSON object with sourced strength numbers. Never estimate numbers yourself; leave it blank where no source gives figures.

```json
{"basis":"reported_actual","as_of":"1944-06-16","vehicles":{"tanks":85},"vehicle_count_type":"operational","source":"Wikipedia Operation Perch","note":"..."}
```

(`personnel` is omitted when the source gives no personnel figure; the example is illustrative and shows the 21st Panzer Division row C0090.)

- `basis`: `reported_actual` = a unit report or an operational count relayed by the source (for example working-order tank returns); `authorized_organization` = establishment / table-of-organisation strength; `estimate` = rounded, approximate or retrospective figures, and landing tallies.
- `as_of`: the date the figure applies to, which can differ from the row date.
- `personnel`: integer number of people, as stated by the source.
- `vehicles`: object of vehicle name to integer count. Only counted vehicles (tanks, assault guns, half-tracks). Guns are not vehicles and are left out.
- `vehicle_count_type`: `operational` (working order), `on_hand` (on hand, working order not stated) or `unknown` (the source does not say what the count means). Required when `vehicles` is present.
- `scope` (**required whenever `personnel` is given**; the validator reports an error if it is missing): `whole_unit`, `sub_force` or `sector`. If no source says what a figure covers, do not record the personnel figure (C0018: sources give 7,000, 8,500 and about 12,000 for the 6th Airborne Division without saying what each includes). A figure given for a line, a task force or a sector (CMH: Kraiss "had 2,500 men, 14 guns ... 5 tanks" for the new Elle line, C0057) is **not** the strength of the whole division: mark it `sub_force` or `sector`, say so in `note`, and do not set a `status` as if it were a whole-unit count unless a source says so. Do not subtract it from, or compare it with, an establishment figure.
- `source`: short source reference. `note`: optional caveat or a conflicting figure from another source (for example 17,200 on paper versus 12,000 for D-Day).
- Only fields with sourced numbers are included. Where sources conflict, the row's `notes` or the `note` field names the other figure.
- `status` (`full`, `reduced` or `heavily_depleted`) is the qualitative strength a source states, set only when a source states strength or losses; it is deliberately sparse. A record with a `status` needs no `personnel` or `vehicles` figure. The old `strength_status` column is derived (`destroyed` when the claim's `destroyed` flag is true, else the `status`, else `unknown`) and appears in `build/all_claims.csv`.

In the proof of concept 20 of 212 rows carry figures (15 estimate, 5 reported_actual, 0 authorized_organization), mostly operational tank counts for panzer divisions and landing tallies for Allied formations. This is a sparse, sourced field, not a full order-of-battle strength table.

## Writing a row: one action, documented wording

- **One row, one action.** A row says what a unit is documented as doing on one date at one place. If the sources describe several actions on that date at different places or against different opponents, write one row per action (new `claim_id`s are allowed; ids are never reused) or, when the pieces cannot be separated, describe the connected action and say in `notes` what is and is not documented. Do not put a day's whole story in `current_activity` (review finding, C0184: a company of Panzer IVs was documented in a three-part fight).
- **Say only what the sources say.** `current_activity` uses the sources' own terms; it does not add a direction, aim or outcome the source does not give. "Attacking from the Orne bridgehead" was overstated for I Corps on 12 June, when the attack was postponed, limited and contained, and the bridgehead itself was under German attack (C0084). Words such as "probably", "likely" or "inferred" belong in `notes`, not in `current_activity`; the validator warns about them and about activities over 120 characters.
- **Name the real opponent.** `engaged_with` lists units a source places in the fight at that place and time, not the formation next door that a source mentions in a different action (C0184: the Mouen fight was against 11th Armoured Division elements, the Grainville attack by the 15th (Scottish) Division was a different action). More than three opponents is a prompt to check whether the row mixes actions.
- **Organisational level.** When a sub-unit did the fighting (a company, a battle group), the row for the parent unit records that element's part and says so in `notes`.
- **Coordinates.** A coordinate must lie within `precision_km` of what the row says it represents. A line or sector is represented by a midpoint with a radius that covers it, not by one end (C0057), and the two rows of a conflict group should not be farther apart than their radii unless the disagreement is about place and says so (C0131 was left near La Haye-du-Puits while C0141 had been corrected to Etienville).

## Dates, time and places

- Dates are calendar dates in local historical time; no time of day is recorded (put it in `notes`).
- Place names follow the source; historical names and later renamings are noted in `notes` where known.
- Coordinates are WGS84. A coordinate may be a representative point (see `location_represents` and the "point = X" wording in `location_qualifier` or an area's name) rather than the exact position of the unit.

## Places

`data/places.csv` holds one row per place (id `PL0001` onward, never reused): canonical `name`, `alt_names`, `kind`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `precision_km`, `country`, `region`, `coord_reference_id` and `coord_source_note`. A claim points at its place with `place_id` and keeps its own `latitude`/`longitude`; the validator requires them to equal the place's point unless `coord_override` gives the reason (another geocoder read of the same place, an approximate point) and the claim point is within 5 km of the place's. The rule was chosen because it changes the least: every claim file, the GeoJSON and every consumer of `all_claims.csv` still has a coordinate on each row, while the place table is the one place to correct a name or a point.

How the 421 names became places (`scripts/migrate_places.py`): a leading `vicinity of` and a trailing parenthesis were split off into `location_qualifier`; claims whose first name part (accents, `St`/`Saint`, a leading article ignored) matched and whose points lay within 3 km of one another became one place with the other spellings as `alt_names`; a name that is a sector, area, beach, bridgehead, front, axis or route is its own place of kind `area` or `line` even when its point is the geocoded centre of a town, and two names with different words are never merged just because they share a point. The point of a place is the one most claims used; a claim with a different read keeps it with a `coord_override`. `docs/places_review.md` lists the merges and the co-located places that were left separate for a decision.

**Merging two places later.** The survivor keeps its id and its point; the other id is retired and recorded in `data/place_merges.csv` (retired id, survivor, the old name, why), never reused. Every claim at the retired place moves to the survivor and keeps its own `precision_km`, `coord_override` and point; whatever made it a separate place (a district, a sector, a name of the time) goes to the claim's `location_qualifier`, so the derived `location_name` still tells it, and a name worth searching for becomes an alias of the survivor. The survivor's `coord_reference_id` stays (it must still appear in the `reference_ids` of a claim at the place). Names and aliases identify one place each. The five merges of item 8 (branch `place-merges`) were made this way by `scripts/migrate_place_merges.py`.

**Coordinate provenance.** The tail `; coords: ...` of `source` (all 566 claims) is gone: the place carries `coord_reference_id` (R001 Wikipedia article coordinates, R002 OpenStreetMap Nominatim, both references of `source_type` `gazetteer_geocoder`) and `coord_source_note` (the article or query as the claims recorded it); a point worked out by hand (the midpoint of two towns, a commune centroid) has a blank `coord_reference_id` and the explanation in the note; a claim with its own point keeps its source in `coord_override`. `claim_reference_roles.csv` keeps only judgments: the role `location` of a `gazetteer_geocoder` reference in a claim's `reference_ids` is derived by `build_references.py` (561 rows were removed), and the claim -> reference links in `build/claim_references.csv` are unchanged.

## Derived values

Values that follow from other data are not stored in the claim files; `common.load_all_claims()` (so the validator, the audits and `build.py`) rebuilds them and `build/all_claims.csv` carries them: `location_name` (the place name with `location_qualifier`: `vicinity` becomes `vicinity of <name>`, the rest goes in parentheses), `location_temporal_offset_days` (`location_date` minus `date`; 0 of 354 rows differed), `strength_status` (see Strength) and the place columns `place_name`, `country`, `region`, `coord_reference_id`, `coord_source_note`. The three lifecycle flags `established`, `disestablished` and `destroyed` are kept: they record what a source says about the unit on that row and are not derivable (`established` is true on a row with event type `defense`, six `activation` rows have it false, and the two `destruction` rows carry a note that the flags follow a Wikipedia infobox, not an order).

## Review guardrails

`python scripts/validate.py` checks structure and, since the first spot check, also reports **errors** for `strength_json` with `personnel` but no `scope` (exit code 1) and prints **warnings** (never errors; exit code unchanged) for: an `attached` or `operational_control` entry that is not the `parent_unit_id`; an `attached` entry without a standing entry (`organic`, `assigned`, `unspecified_subordination` or `unknown`); `organic` used for a division, corps or army under a higher headquarters; a named higher headquarters without a unit id; a relationship whose `effective_to` is before the row's date; a `location_date` that differs from `date` without a temporal `location_basis`; a corps, army or army-group headquarters that is only a town centroid with precision under 5 km; a corps or army sector recorded as an exact `front_line_point`; a conflict group whose rows have different event types but no `Event definition:` in notes; (errors for an offset that does not match `location_date` and for a landing row taking its coordinate from a town centroid or a CP of another date); a relationship whose `effective_from` is after the row's date; a `current_activity` over 120 characters or with a hedge word; more than three opponents in `engaged_with` and `engaged_note` together; and a non-alternate claim dated before the unit's `formed_date` or after an end row (`destruction`, or `destroyed` / `disestablished`) or a lineage successor date (`check_lifespan`; an activation after an end starts a new life, alternates are ignored, `absorbed_into` is not the successor's formation, a 3-day tail after an end and a 7-day window around a lineage date are allowed). The one current lifespan warning is Jutland `C8908` (roadmap 370(a)). Existing rows that trigger a warning are a to-do list for the next review pass, not a defect in the check.

`python scripts/check_coordinates.py` prints coordinate warnings (it exits 0): points outside a theater bounding box, a point in the open Atlantic (a place or a claim), a claim more than 3 km from its place's point, conflict-group claims farther apart than their precision radii, a unit that moves more than 80 km/day between preferred rows, a claim whose point does not match its place (`override`: a `coord_override` point farther from the place point than the claim's own `precision_km`, or a point more than 50 m off the place point without a `coord_override`), and a place point farther than max(15 km, twice `precision_km`) from every geocoder candidate already stored for its name (`place-geocoder`; offline, it reads `.cache/geocode.json` when that file exists, compares kind `point` places only, and checks nothing where the cache is absent, as in CI; `--places` fills the cache for every place, one request per second). With `--geocode` (add `--country fr` for a French campaign) it also fetches up to ten geocode candidates (OpenStreetMap Nominatim, one request per second, cached in `.cache/`) for the place named in `location_name` (the place name and qualifier) and warns when even the nearest candidate is farther than max(15 km, twice `precision_km`) from the point. Run it before a release and after adding many rows; it is not run in CI. In its first full run (212 rows) the geocode check flagged 4 rows, all false positives caused by name matching (a place written without accents, short names such as Vire and Caumont, an ambiguous Pont-l'Abbe), and could not geocode about 45 sector, line and axis names at all, so treat it as a prompt only and read each warning against the row; the offline conflict check, in the same run, found the C0131/C0141 disagreement and, later, a 49 km gap in conflict group G004 (C0067/C0081) that came from a placeholder point and a mislabelled event place, not from a real place disagreement (both rows now sit at Saint-Lô and nearby Hébécrevon). The check has no allowance list: a gap must be fixed or recorded in `notes` as a deliberate place disagreement. A warning means "look at this row", and a deliberate place disagreement or an `approach_route` row can be a correct reason to keep the data. Limits of `place-geocoder`: it compares a place with the geocoder's other candidates, so it finds a hand-edited point, a point of the wrong namesake (it flagged Pont-l'Abbe, now an explained exception because the stored point is right) or a point moved by a typing error, but it cannot find a point that IS the geocoder's first hit and is the wrong object (the street, shop or wrong namesake of V-003, V-004, V-015 to V-017 would not have been flagged by it); that needs a second, independent source such as the Wikipedia coordinates of the town, which is not automated yet. The three speed warnings that remain open (C0253, C0419, C0480) are consecutive-day pairs of the CMH command-post lists (4th Armored Division: 14 August Pouance (7 mi E), 15 August Ste-Cerotte, 140 km; 30th Division: 14 August Barenton, 15 August La Martiniere, 127 km; 94th Division: 8 September Ste-Marie-du-Mont, 9 September Plouay (2 mi S), 225 km). The dataset reproduces the CMH entries and dates, and no second source was found for them, so they stay open as prompts: either the division moved that far in a day, or the list's dates or vicinity points are coarse; no data were changed for them.

## Spot-check review

Spot checks (sampling about 15 rows, ideally by someone who did not write them) find different problems from the validator. Choose the sample by risk, not at random only: all rows with an `attached` or `operational_control` entry, rows with `strength_json`, rows in a conflict group, rows whose `current_activity` is long, and rows near the end of a long march. For each sampled row, apply the per-row checklist in [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md#per-row-review-checklist) against a source that was **not** used to write the row, record the outcome (confirmed / corrected / disputed) and the change in a review note, and fix only what the sources support. The first review (five of 15 rows corrected) is the origin of the rules above.

## Provenance of the first import

The proof of concept was built in five iterations (v1 to v5): v1 flat claims; v2 added unit-name/nationality/echelon split, operation and sub-operation names, `precision_km`, numeric `confidence` and conflict groups; v3 added `current_activity`; v4 added `unit_id`, `parent_formation`, `commander`, `event_type`, `engaged_with` and `strength_status`; v5 added battle groups as units and `strength_json`; this repository adds `theater`, splits the data by unit and replaces the free-text `parent_formation` by `parent_unit_id` + `command_relationships` (`scripts/migrate_parent_formation.py`). The flat v5 CSVs are kept in `archive/v5_flat/`, and `scripts/split_from_flat.py` reproduces `data/` from them, including that last step (the flat files keep the original `parent_formation` text). `claim_id`s are identical in every version.

## Batch 2: command transitions, 25 July to 15 September 1944

- **Corps and army command-post places.** CMH's own corps and army command-post lists were not reachable from the box (history.army.mil blocks automated fetches). The World War II by Day atlas (`ww2byday.com/<id>/mapunit/`) states that its locations come from the CMH ETO order of battle and gives a dated list per corps, army and army group. Those lists are used as a secondary compilation: a place on a listed date is `documented_on_date` at confidence 0.5 (one compilation; 0.7 only where a Green Book or memorial text agrees); a row between two listed dates is `nearest_earlier_command_post` or `nearest_later_command_post` with the usual 1-2 / 3-5 / over-5-day bands.
- **Event rows versus headquarters rows.** An attack or command-change row whose subject is a sector keeps a sector point (`operational_area`, `sector_center`) even where the headquarters is known; the headquarters place gets its own `hq_move` row.
- **Sequences, not conflicts.** Where two dates describe different events (2nd Armored Division to XIX Corps on 19 August versus XIX Corps taking over the Mantes bridgehead on 29 August, with a one-day XV Corps entry between) each event is its own `command_change` row with an `Event definition:` note; no conflict group is used. A conflict group is used only where two sources give different places for the same event; First Army on 1 August (Vouilly or Saint-Gilles) was one in Batch 2 and was re-modelled as a move in sequence after the review (group G015 dissolved).
- **Validator.** The warning "an attached/operational_control entry exists but parent_unit_id is not one of them" no longer fires when the attachment is to a headquarters of higher echelon than the parent (a division in a corps zone that the CMH table shows attached to First Army): the corps remains the immediate parent.

## Batch 2 review corrections

A reviewer's findings on the 15-row Batch 2 spot check were treated as unverified and checked against the sources; the outcomes by claim are in [`review/review_changes.md`](review/review_changes.md). Rules that came out of it:

- **A relationship has an interval.** `valid_from` / `valid_to` of the relation bound it; a missing end is not "forever" (see "Validity of a relationship" in `schema/README.md`). Order date and effective date are separate (`ordered_date`). A date range is `valid_from` with `valid_from_latest`. An end that no source dates is closed at the unit's next claim, says so in `valid_to_basis` and sets `end_unknown`.
- **Temporary control is not reassignment.** Where a narrative describes a short command of a formation inside an operation (XV Corps over the 2nd Armored Division in the Mantes bridgehead, 28-29 August) the corps is `operational_control` with an end date, even if the CMH single corps column shows the corps. A standing link that is only implied by the later return stays in the row with `date_basis` `inferred` and a note saying which source is missing.
- **A retained function is not control.** A source saying a headquarters kept supply, administration or civil affairs in an area does not make it `administrative_control` of a formation; use `unknown` with a scoped note.
- **Map against text (Scott, 9 November 2026).** When a dated text row and a 12th Army Group situation-map row of the same unit are more than 25 km apart (beyond both precisions) within a day, they form a conflict group with the text row preferred and the map row alternate. A weak text row (representative point, operational area, inferred, another date's command post, objective, Wikipedia only) is not preferred this way; the pair goes to the roadmap (357).
- **A sequence is not a conflict.** Two headquarters places that can both be true on adjacent days (First Army at Vouilly until 2 August, at Saint-Gilles from 1 August) are rows in sequence; a conflict group needs claims that cannot both hold.
- **Objective points.** A row whose point is an objective (`location_represents` objective) and where the unit was not there on the date uses `location_basis` `representative_point` and says so in the name; `documented_on_date` is kept only with an `Objective check:` sentence.
- **Temporary components are rows of their own.** A combat command transferred without its division (CCA, 2nd Armored, to XIX Corps at noon on 28 July) has its own claim; the corps' `composing_units` and `composing_text` date each entry and do not read as an organic order of battle.
- **Event support is separate from location support** in the notes of command rows (see the confidence discussion in `schema/README.md`); no new numeric column.
- **Coordinate checks.** The speed check counts days between the dates of the two coordinates; checked fast moves are listed with a source in `data/coordinate_exceptions.csv` and reported as explained.

## Batch 3: October to December 1944

- **Scope.** Milestones only (command changes, new headquarters places, first combat or arrival of newly active units, the start of major offensives); 48 claims. Skipped events and uncovered units are listed in `docs/roadmap.md`, "Batch 3".
- **Relationship dates.** Army and army-group attachments from the CMH tables (`assignment_table`) are closed with `effective_to` where a later table row no longer lists them (the day before that row), with `effective_to_basis` `assignment_table`; narrative dates (Green Book) take precedence where they conflict with a table date. The 20 December change of First and Ninth Armies to the British 21st Army Group is `operational_control` with ends read from the tables (First Army 17 January 1945, Ninth Army 3 April 1945); the 12th Army Group `assigned` entry is kept because the tables keep it.
- **Same-day changes.** When a source dates a change "during the 20th" without an hour (VIII Corps passing to Third Army), both the old and the new relationship are listed for that date and the hour is left undocumented; a CMH table with two rows on one date (84th Division, 20 December) is modelled as a same-day end and start.
- **101st Airborne Division, 17-21 December.** Originally conflict group G016 (Green Book 20 December against the CMH table's 17 December); after the Batch 3 review the rows are a sequence (table observation, VIII Corps operational control from 18 December, VIII Corps to Third Army during 20 December) and the group is dissolved. The derived `command_relations.csv` still ignores alternate and disputed rows so the preferred row decides.
- **Corps places.** Corps headquarters places come from the World War II by Day command-post lists, as in Batch 2. A row taking its place from a neighbour (XVIII Airborne Corps at the 82nd's command post) says so in `Location support:`. A headquarters place more than three days from its list entry is 0.3, whatever the Green Book says about the neighbourhood.
- **Sector points.** Offensive-start rows for corps use `sector_center` (precision 10) where a Green Book page names a line of departure or a crossing, and a headquarters point (precision 5-10) where it does not.

## Batch 3 review corrections and temporal audit

An outside review of the Batch 3 spot check was treated as unverified and checked against the Green Books (Ardennes, Siegfried Line, Riviera to the Rhine) and the CMH tables; the outcome by claim, the points where the sources disagree with the review and the declined proposals are in [`review/review_changes.md`](review/review_changes.md). Rules that came out of it:

- **A table row is an observation, not an event.** `subordinate_assignment` (a table or order lists the unit under a headquarters from a date) and `hq_observation` (a command-post list records the headquarters at a place) are separate event types from `activation` and `hq_move`. `activation` is used only where a source documents the operational start, such as a corps taking its first sector (XIII Corps, 8 November).
- **Sequences carry the hour question.** `effective_to` is inclusive; on a boundary day both entries cover the day; optional `effective_time` / `effective_to_time` carry a documented hour; `who_controls.py` answers UNKNOWN where an hour is needed and missing.
- **Immediate parent, then hierarchy.** `parent_unit_id` is the immediate operational parent (the 101st's parent is VIII Corps from 18 December, the 84th's is the XVIII Airborne Corps until the VII Corps takes over); the army is read from the corps' rows.
- **Components get rows.** The Reserve Combat Command of the 4th Armored Division (`US-CCR-004D`) has its own row for the relief of Bastogne; the division row's point is a representative point for the place its spearhead captured.
- **Operations as a table.** `operation_id` replaces the free-text `campaign`, `operation_name` and `sub_operation_name`. `data/operations.csv` holds the CMH campaign streamers, the Overlord umbrella, operations, battles and phases with a parent chain, dates, a `date_basis` and sources; `data/operation_relations.csv` holds the non-hierarchical links and `data/operation_exceptions.csv` the claims dated just outside their operation. `build/all_claims.csv` derives `operation`, `campaign`, `operation_name`, `sub_operation_name` and `operation_path` from the chain, so the old names remain available, and `operation_name` is still blank outside the operation's dates (Overlord no longer labels rows after 30 August 1944). The migration kept the old labels where the date fits (280 rows), kept 11 rows a few days outside the dates with an exception, put 252 rows with only a campaign label on the campaign row, and left 20 unmatched rows and 3 two-label rows on a chosen row or the campaign; they are listed in [`review/operations_backfill.md`](review/operations_backfill.md). A claim belonging to two operations at once was not found (the 3 two-label rows are an operation and the operation it supported, so one is chosen and the other linked by a relation), so there is no `claim_operations` table.
- **Claims store their references.** Each claim lists `reference_ids` (and, where a page is known, `source_locator`) and `build/claim_references.csv` is built from them; before, the link was derived each time by matching the URLs in `source`, which could not hold a locator that was not in a URL, could not say what a source supported and broke when a URL was edited. The design mirrors `operation_id`: the claim file stays the master and holds the keys (`reference_ids`, `source_locator`); the reference rows live in `data/references_meta.csv`; what is not a property of the claim alone, the *role* of a link, lives in a side table `data/claim_reference_roles.csv` with a stated `basis`. A one-table design (`data/claim_references.csv` as the master with claim-file columns derived) was rejected: a contributor would edit two files to cite one source, per-unit files would no longer show their own sources, and claim rows and links could drift without any file showing it. Roles (`event`, `date`, `location`, `command_relationship`, `strength`, `general`) are recorded only where the claim's own text gives evidence (the coordinate note names the service, a `strength_json` source, a source named in the Event date, Event definition, Location or Relationship sentence of `notes`); everything else is `general`, so most links carry no role claim. The old `source` text stays as the readable citation and `validate.py` fails when a URL in it does not resolve to an id in `reference_ids`.
- **Independent sources.** A claim at confidence 0.9 should rest on at least two source families (`source_family` in `data/references_meta.csv`). Wikipedia in any language is one family, the US Army Center of Military History (CMH) and the sites that republish it are another, a geocoder is its own family, and any other site is its own host. This is conservative: it treats a Green Book and the Wikipedia article that summarises it as two families, because the article has its own editors and other sources, and it does not look behind the family label to see whether one source copied another. The count ignores references of `source_type` `gazetteer_geocoder` (a geocoder shows where a place is, not that the unit was there); before the places table it counted them. All 9 claims at 0.9 have two or more families under both counts (the rubric warning list is empty before and after); across all 566 claims 90 had a single family under the old count and 391 have one under the current count (mostly Wikipedia or CMH plus a coordinate service), which is information, not an error, because the rubric asks for two families only at 0.9.
- **Operation boundaries are contested.** The CMH streamer dates are used for campaigns. Operation dates come from the official histories (the Green Books) or Wikipedia and carry a `date_basis`; where they differ the row says so. Cobra sits under the Northern France campaign (it opened on 25 July, the streamer's first day), not Normandy; Overlord ends on 30 August 1944; Pursuit across France has derived dates. The full list is in [`review/operations_backfill.md`](review/operations_backfill.md).
- **Operations are added when a claim needs them.** No row exists for the Hedgerows, the Hurtgen Forest, the Colmar Pocket or Nordwind, because no claim points at them yet; add them with the procedure in CONTRIBUTING.md when claims arrive.
- **Temporal audit.** `python scripts/audit_temporal_relationships.py` reads all claims together and lists twelve temporal patterns (nine claim patterns and, from the master table, documented contradictory overlaps, relations with no sourced end and undated parent changes) (overlapping holders of one echelon, parent not in force, restated start dates that disagree, a pair that is closed and relisted, undated parent changes, echelon skips, undated transfers, same-day place conflicts, conflict groups that are really sequences). It is read-only and its output is a prompt to check sources. The first run found 17 findings: 4 real and fixed by 3 edits (the XV Corps date to Seventh Army, 29 September not 15 September, and two transfer dates held only in a note), 7 real and left open (five undated Batch 1 parent changes, one coverage gap, one possible sequence recorded as a conflict) and 6 explained patterns ([`review/review_changes.md`](review/review_changes.md)). The CI step is non-failing.
- **Composition columns (October 2026).** `composing_units` is a `;` list of `unit_id`s (or `unknown`; blank together with a blank `composing_text` means unchanged from the unit's previous row) for subordinates that are a unit of `data/units.csv`. Everything else (regiments, battalions, companies and groups that are not units yet, remarks) is free text in `composing_text`, as it was written; an entry `<unit_id> (<qualifier>)` carries the qualifier of a listed unit ('GER-PZ-002D (elements)', 'US-INF-030D (from noon 28 July)'). An entry never repeats the name of a unit that has an id, and never holds an id inside a sentence (a unit with an id is always `<unit_id> (qualifier)` and listed in `composing_units`; review item 3), and never contains the word "attach" in any form (attached, attachment, ...; also in a qualifier; the validator rejects it in `composing_text` and `engaged_note`): how a unit is joined to another is a relation in `data/command_relations.csv`, or a row of `docs/deferred_relations.md` when the formation is not a unit of the table (review items 1 and 2; the narrative columns `notes` and `current_activity` keep the word where it is the source's own description). `build/all_claims.csv` derives `composing_units_names` (the unit name, or the display name when the nation differs from the claiming unit's, plus qualifiers, then the text). See `docs/unit_references_review.md`.
- **Normalization: cheap fixes (October 2026).** Applied by `scripts/migrate_normalize.py` after the review in `docs/normalization_review.md`: (1) command-relationship entries no longer store the formation's `name` (derived from `units.csv`; `higher_name` in `build/command_relations.csv` is now always `<adjective> <unit_name>`), an entry with `unit_id` null is only `unknown` with a note, and the `unknown` sentinel in `parent_unit_id` / `command_relationships` is replaced by a blank `parent_unit_id` plus one explicit unknown entry; (2) `parent_only` was dropped from `units.csv` (it equalled `record_status = placeholder`); (3) `data/nationalities.csv` is the lookup table of nations (the three `Allied` units keep that explicit value; the operation scope `German` is now `Germany`); (4) echelon rank, relationship groups, source types and the operation vocabularies live only in `schema/vocabularies.json`; (5) foreign keys are declared in the schemas and enforced, and `theater` joined the repeated unit columns that must equal `units.csv`. Larger items of the review (a places table, a command-relations master table, ids in `engaged_with` / `composing_units`, a branch/echelon split) are not part of this change.
- **German end rows: mid-war first (Scott, 9 November 2026).** Triage rule: a unit is *mid-war* when the existing claims, lineage or the Lexikon der Wehrmacht put its destruction, disbandment, absorption or redesignation before about April 1945; *war's end* when it went into captivity or dissolved in the April-May 1945 surrenders or pockets (Ruhr, Harz, Alps, the Elbe, the Enns, Halbe and so on), or when its claims run to April 1945 or later with no earlier end; *unknown* otherwise. Mid-war ends get an end row: the date and place a source gives; if the source gives a date but no place, the row sits at the unit's last recorded place or the named region at the coarsest fitting precision, with a `Location caveat` sentence; if it gives no day, no row (roadmap 372). A unit re-formed under the same `unit_id` has not ended. Battle groups and battalions get end rows only when their parent chain is traceable and a source dates their end.
