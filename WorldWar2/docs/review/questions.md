# Ten questions for an outside view

These are the design choices on which an outside reader's judgement is most useful to us. Each says what the dataset does now and where to look. We would like, for each: what works, what would break or mislead, what you would do differently and why, and an example from the data where you can. Please answer in the findings format (area `Q1` ... `Q10`) or in a few sentences each. The questions are open; none of them assumes the current choice is right.

1. **Place model.** Claims point to a place table (`data/places.csv`: a point, area or line with a canonical name, aliases, coordinates and a coordinate source) and add a `location_qualifier`, an optional `coord_override`, a `precision_km` from a fixed set, and `location_represents` (what the point stands for). Is this a sound way to record "where was the unit" without implying precision the sources do not give? What would you change (for example areas as polygons, separate unit-position and place tables, a gazetteer identifier)? See `schema/README.md`, "places columns", and `docs/places_review.md`.

2. **Claim key and conflicts.** A claim is identified by `claim_id`; `(unit_id, date, event_type)` must be unique, with two ways to repeat a key (a conflict group of disagreeing sources, or a listed exception for different actions of one kind on one day). Competing claims are separate rows with `claim_status` preferred, alternate or disputed. Is this key the right one? Does the preferred / alternate / disputed status give a user a safe default? See `CONTRIBUTING.md`, "Claim identity", and `docs/methodology.md`, "Conflict policy".

3. **Command-relationship vocabulary and model.** Ten relationship values (`organic`, `assigned`, `attached`, `unspecified_subordination`, `operational_control`, `administrative_control`, `supporting`, `coordinating`, `reserve`, `unknown`), dated inclusive intervals with a basis for each end, claims as evidence, documented contradictory overlaps that are not resolved. Are the values and definitions usable and sufficient for 1944 practice? Is the interval model with bases right, or would states or events serve better? See `schema/README.md`, "Command relations".

4. **Confidence rubric.** Four levels applied by rule from location exactness and the number of independent source families, with caps for conflicted claims; confidence is about date and place, not about the event. Is this a useful measure? Would separate scores (date, place, event), a numeric score, or a different set of levels be better? Is "independent source family" a workable notion? See `docs/methodology.md`, "Confidence rubric".

5. **Branch and echelon split.** Units have an `echelon` (size) and a `branch` (kind: Army, Armoured, Airborne, Waffen-SS, Luftwaffe, and reserved values for Marines, Air Force and Navy), a nationality, and an id that encodes nationality, type, number and an echelon letter. Is this a clean model of unit identity and type? How should things such as an armoured division, a battle group, a combat command, a corps-level troop formation or a re-designated unit be represented? See `schema/README.md`, "units columns" and "unit_id pattern".

6. **Commanders.** The commander is a free-text column of the claim (rank and name as the source gives it, with a note for a temporary commander, or `unknown`); there is no commanders table. Is that adequate for research use? What would a commanders table need (persons, appointments with dates, sources) and when does it pay off?

7. **Evidence model.** A claim cites its sources in `reference_ids` and `source_locator`; `claim_reference_roles` records which part of a claim a source supports where that matters; `notes` carries labelled sentences (`Event support:`, `Location support:`, `Relationship:`) about what is documented. A table with one row per claim, aspect (event, date, location, relationship, strength), statement, reference and locator has been considered and not built. Would you build it now, later, or not? What does the current form lose or hide? See `schema/README.md`, "References", and `docs/normalization_review.md`.

8. **Scaling to January to May 1945.** The remaining US Army months would be added in batches (a milestone pass each, with a review gate), at the current structure; the number of claims would grow substantially. Which parts of the design, the checks or the process will strain first (ids, conflicts, relations, placeholders, review effort, the validator, the size of the CSV files)? What would you add before starting?

9. **Air and naval extension.** The vocabularies reserve air force, air formation, fleet and naval unit echelons and Air Force and Navy branches. Would the place model, the claim and the relationship model carry units that move fast, are tracked by sorties or voyages, or have no fixed headquarters? What would need a different table?

10. **Recording of licences and source terms.** The dataset keeps, per source, a licence or status field and a reliability note (`data/references.csv`), and a data licence and code licence for the repository as a whole. (The choice of licence is not under review.) Is it clear enough from the data what applies to a row and what a downstream user must do (for example share-alike for Wikipedia-derived facts, public-domain US Army histories, attribution for a geocoder)? What is missing for a reuser to comply with confidence?
