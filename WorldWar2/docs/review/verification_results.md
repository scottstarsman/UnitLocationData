## 2. What was checked, and how

**Claims.** For each claim the cited references (`reference_ids`, with `source_locator`) were opened and read at the passage named. Each of the seven checklist items was marked ok, not shown by the cited sources ("uncovered"), or wrong: unit, date, event (the `event_type` and `current_activity`), place (the name, and the point against `precision_km`), commander and command relationships, support (does every cited source support something, is any fact unsupported by all), and confidence recomputed from the rubric in `schema/vocabularies.json`.

**Relations.** For each relation the cited pages and tables were read for the higher unit, the relationship, the start and the end, and the evidence claims (`evidence_claim_ids`) were read to see that they state what the table says. The start and end `basis`, `end_unknown` and `change_undated` were compared with what the sources show.

**Verdict rule.** `supported`: every checked item holds in the cited sources (or in a source that the row's own notes name and that was opened and read). `partly supported`: at least one item fails, or is not shown by any of the cited sources, while the others hold; the comment says which item. `not supported`: a source contradicts the row. `unverifiable`: no cited source could be opened, or none covers the claim at all. A row whose single weak item was a detail that a notes-named source settles was kept `supported` and the comment says so ("citation note"); a row where the settling source is cited nowhere on the row, or where the cited source disagrees, is `partly supported` (class source gap).

**Independent checks.** Where a cited source did not cover an item, one further source was opened to see whether the stored value is right (for example a Wikipedia commander list, the Center of Military History unit page, or place coordinates). That result is recorded as part of the finding and does not change the verdict of the row.

**How a command-relation table was read.** The CMH ETO-OB assignment tables have one row per date with the corps, the army to which it is assigned or attached, and the army group; "(-)" means relieved. A date in a table row is taken as the first day of the entry. A relation that starts on the date of the table row "in force at the unit's first claim", rather than the date the entry began, was marked as a start that differs from the table (the dataset's own rule; see V-013 in the findings).

**Deviation from the checklist.** The checklist says `unverifiable` for a source that "does not cover" the claim. That word was used only for a row where nothing in the sources covers the claim. A source that covers most items but not one of them gives `partly supported`, because the other items could be checked; this is why `unverifiable` is 0 although some items are marked as not shown. An item that was settled only in a source that the row does not cite is described as a source gap in the findings.

**Who checked.** One checker made all 60 verdicts; no second person has read them. The verdicts, the quotations and the page locators are in the comment columns and in the table below, so each can be re-checked.

| relations with a wrong parent or relationship | 0 of 20 (0%; 95% interval 0% to 16%) |
| relations with a start or end that differs from a source | 7 of 20 (35%; 95% interval 18% to 57%) |

Why the rates do not carry over: the claims are allocated by nationality (square-root allocation: Canada 2 of 4, Germany 9 of 75, the UK 8 of 60, the US 21 of 427) and chosen to cover every confidence level, echelon, event type and status, so rare values (alternate and disputed claims, the 0.3 and 0.9 levels, rare event types) are over-represented; the 20 relations are one draw from 375 and include `unknown`, `organic`, alternate and disputed rows. The direction of the resulting bias is not known, and a second draw with another seed could give different counts. The boundary problems are spread over June to December and over both Allied and German relations; they are not confined to one source.

## 7. Check of places outside the sample

The two wrong points in the sample (V-003, V-004) were geocoder hits, so the 170 places whose coordinates come from OpenStreetMap (`coord_reference_id` R002) were checked as well; this check is not part of the sample and its results are not in the counts of section 1.

1. **Reproduction.** Each place was searched again with the query recorded in `coord_source_note`. 164 returned the stored point exactly. 6 returned a different point today, because the recorded query is ambiguous (for example "Arpajon, France"); their stored points were compared with other sources: Arpajon (0.0 km from the Wikipedia coordinates), Longnes (0.1 km), Zon/Son (0.8 km from Son en Breugel) and Le Ruel (a hamlet of Haravilliers, Val-d'Oise, by reverse geocoding the stored point) are right; the Leicester point lies in the city and was not checked further; **Boinville (PL0043, claim C0264) was unresolved at this check and was resolved afterwards (commit `5d50d73`, see the status table)**: the stored point is in Châlo-Saint-Mars (Essonne), about 60 km from Boinville-en-Mantois (Yvelines, near the Mantes crossing), and the CMH names only "Boinville, Seine-et-Oise"; which place is meant was not established. For 6 places the query returned no result today (Agay, Biéville-Beuville, Cavalaire-sur-Mer, Lyon, Newbury, Sainte-Maxime); they were not checked.
2. **Kind of object.** 17 stored points are a hit that is not a settlement or an administrative area for the named place (a street, a pub, a church, a castle, a hotel, a memorial, a building, a rail halt, an administrative building). For each, the point was compared with the Wikipedia coordinates of the named town where the article has coordinates. **5 are wrong**: PL0195 and PL0306 (the sample's S23 and S25) and three that are far from the town they name (V-015 to V-017). 12 are right or intended: the named building, or a point within 3 km of the named commune (command posts at châteaux, a hotel, a church, a memorial, Hill 112; the point in the forest of Fontainebleau, 4.7 km from the town centre, is in the forest that the place names; Diarville, Notre-Dame-du-Touchet, La Denisière, Kerdivichen, Versailles, Saint-Sauveur-Lendelin, Creully, Thiville, Groesbeek). Hill 112 and the church at Saint-Denis-du-Gast have no Wikipedia coordinates and were accepted by name.
3. **Not checked.** The 204 places from Wikipedia coordinates (R001) were not compared with anything in this pass. A hit of the right kind can still be the wrong place of that name (as Boinville may be), so a clean result here does not prove the remaining points right.

## 9. What this does not show

- It does not give an error rate for the dataset (section 6).
- The verdicts rest on the cited sources and on the sources named in the comments; where a source is a secondary one (Wikipedia, a ww2byday map), "supported" means the source says it, not that it is right.
- One checker made the judgments of "inferred" and "convention"; a second reader could differ on the five design rows (R13, R14, R16, R17, R19) and on the severity of V-001 to V-004.
- A change of any sampled row regenerates the sample files; the verdict columns are kept by `scripts/review_sample.py` for rows that do not change and are ignored by `--check`.
